Case Study

Upskilling the Public Sector

A Design Thinking sprint exploring how personalized learning and gamification can support digital transformation within the Swiss Federal Administration.

Note: This experimental project explored deliberately disruptive solutions to a real business problem.

What

A concept for a learning platform where employees are encouraged and rewarded for developing the skills the organization actually needs.

Why

Digital transformation has been a strategic priority within the Swiss Federal Administration for decades, yet many organizations still struggle to translate it into concrete action.

How

During a four-day Design Thinking sprint, our interdisciplinary team of four explored ideas beyond the obvious.

Problem

Everyone wants digitalization. Nobody knows exactly what it means.

Unclear direction

Digital transformation is a strategic priority, but a clear vision is often missing.

Limited resources

Employees are expected to drive change with limited resources, unclear expectations and little practical guidance.

Huge scope

The scale of the organization makes digital transformation difficult to coordinate and prioritize.

Solution

Reward people who
drive digitalization.

Instead of outsourcing digital expertise or creating new positions, the concept invests in employees who already understand the organization. A personalized learning platform helps them develop the skills the organization actually needs and rewards their progress.

Clear priorities

Employees can see which skills matter most and where to focus their development.

Personalized learning

Learning paths adapt to individual skill gaps instead of offering the same development journey to everyone.

Motivation to grow

Gamification makes progress visible and rewards employees for developing skills that support the organization.

Impact

A disruptive concept led to a more practical solution.

Sparked

The concept sparked new thinking about how employee skills could be made visible and put to use across the organization.

Reframed

The original gamification idea evolved into a simpler internal skills marketplace: find people with the expertise a project needs and connect with them directly.

Evaluated

This marketplace concept is now being evaluated as a possible way to mobilize hidden skills across teams without relying on outsourcing.

01

Empathize

How do people experience digital transformation?

To better understand the challenges behind digital transformation, each team member conducted interviews with employees across different roles within the Swiss Federal Administration. Together, we collected perspectives from 4 managers, 5 employees and 1 subject matter expert. Key Decision: Interviews were conducted online, so they could be automatically transcribed by Microsoft Teams. AI could then help extract key insights before the team reviewed and synthesized the findings together.

Interview Material

Prompting in ChatGPT to extract insights from interview transcripts

02

Define

Finding the right problem to solve.

Interview findings were grouped into themes using affinity mapping. Together, we identified recurring pain points, challenged our assumptions with AI, and translated the strongest opportunities into "How Might We" questions:

How might we map employees' skills?
How might personalized learning experiences look?
How might employees be guided through this transformation journey? Key Decision: The challenge was broad and the timeline exceptionally short, so we narrowed our focus to employee skills. We set aside larger organizational challenges, such as replacing widely used digital tools and strengthening management support and strategic leadership.

Digital meets analog.

03

Ideate

Exploring as many ideas as possible.

Ideas were generated collaboratively and enriched with AI using techniques such as the Morphological Box and Random Word. The concepts were then discussed, voted on and evaluated against predefined criteria before we refined the strongest solution.

Random Word Method, Morphological box and prioritization

04

Prototype

20 minutes to bring the concept to life.

With the deadline approaching, we split up to make the concept tangible. While part of the team refined the personalized learning concept, I focused on creating an interactive prototype of the proposed skills management platform. Key Decision: As the team member responsible for the prototype of the skills management platform, I made all design decisions, from writing the initial prompt to refining the result into a functional prototype.

Prompting in Claude

20-minute prototype

05

Test

A reality check made us hesitate.

The prototype was presented to another team to gather feedback. Rather than discussing the interface itself, the conversation quickly shifted to organizational adoption and the barriers that could prevent such a platform from succeeding. Transparency and security concerns would require organizational change management and clear communication about how employee data is used. Key Outcome: Introducing fixed financial rewards for developing skills would have been difficult to implement, as they would have required a dedicated budget. However, when I presented the idea to my division, there was strong interest in an internal skills marketplace where employees’ hidden expertise could be made visible and put to use on projects across the organization, reducing the need for outsourcing.
